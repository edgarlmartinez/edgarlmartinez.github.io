About

Images are simulated lateral radiographs made from public VerSe 2019 CT scans (Sekuboyina et al.; Löffler et al.; CC BY-SA 4.0). These derived images are shared under the same license. Landmarks come from a small model trained only on other VerSe cases. The purple ghost points are the CT-derived reference landmarks. Level names are taken from the reference for this demo. Height loss and Genant grade

Six points per body give the anterior (Ha), middle (Hm) and posterior (Hp) heights. Expected normal heights are interpolated from the nearest non-fractured vertebra above and below, weighted by level distance, or taken from one side at the ends of the field. Loss % = 1 − measured ÷ expected for each height. The largest loss sets the semiquantitative Genant grade: under 20% normal, 20–25% grade 1, 25–40% grade 2, over 40% grade 3. The morphology comes from the most reduced height: anterior means wedge, middle means biconcave, posterior means crush. C1 and C2 are not graded.

Retropulsion (posterior wall displacement)

PWD is how far the posterior wall of the vertebral body sits behind the line joining the posterior-wall midpoints of the vertebrae above and below, in mm. Positive values mean it bulges toward the canal, and normal walls are usually 0–3 mm. Canal % compares the sagittal canal diameter (posterior wall midpoint to the spinolaminar point) with the mean of the adjacent levels. On a radiograph this is a midline sagittal estimate only. It can't give canal area or show fragments off the midline, and CT stays the reference.

Calibration

Demo images are 1.0 mm/px parallel projections, so there's no magnification. For your own images, enter the detector pixel spacing corrected for magnification. Percentages and ratios don't depend on calibration.
